References

The textbooks, fundamental constants and data sources behind the spectrum, the seven types of radiation and every interactive figure. Each entry notes where it is used in the module.

Sources

  1. Skoog, D. A.; Holler, F. J.; Crouch, S. R. Principles of Instrumental Analysis, 7th ed.; Cengage Learning: Boston, MA, 2018.Used for: region boundaries; infrared, ultraviolet and X-ray sources (Globar, Nernst glower, deuterium lamp, X-ray tube); NMR, XRF, XRD and gamma-ray methods.
  2. Pavia, D. L.; Lampman, G. M.; Kriz, G. S.; Vyvyan, J. R. Introduction to Spectroscopy, 5th ed.; Cengage Learning: Stamford, CT, 2015.Used for: the IR correlation table, the NMR spectrum of ethanol, and UV-vis absorption of conjugated polyenes.
  3. Atkins, P.; de Paula, J.; Keeler, J. Atkins’ Physical Chemistry, 11th ed.; Oxford University Press: Oxford, 2018.Used for: blackbody radiation and Wien’s law, rotational spectra, the hydrogen spectrum, and the vibrational modes of CO2.
  4. Tiesinga, E.; Mohr, P. J.; Newell, D. B.; Taylor, B. N. CODATA Recommended Values of the Fundamental Physical Constants: 2018. Rev. Mod. Phys. 2021, 93 (2), 025010. doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.93.025010Used for: the speed of light, Planck, Avogadro, Boltzmann, Stefan–Boltzmann and Wien constants and the electron volt.
  5. Linstrom, P. J.; Mallard, W. G., Eds. NIST Chemistry WebBook, NIST Standard Reference Database 69; National Institute of Standards and Technology: Gaithersburg, MD. webbook.nist.gov/chemistryUsed for: the rotational constant of CO and the vibrational frequencies of CO2.
  6. Kramida, A.; Ralchenko, Yu.; Reader, J.; NIST ASD Team. NIST Atomic Spectra Database; National Institute of Standards and Technology: Gaithersburg, MD. physics.nist.gov/asdUsed for: the hydrogen, sodium and mercury emission lines.
  7. Deslattes, R. D.; Kessler, E. G., Jr.; Indelicato, P.; de Billy, L.; Lindroth, E.; Anton, J. X-ray Transition Energies: New Approach to a Comprehensive Evaluation. Rev. Mod. Phys. 2003, 75 (1), 35–99. doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.75.35Used for: Kα and Kβ wavelengths and K-shell energies of copper, molybdenum and chromium.
  8. National Nuclear Data Center. NuDat 3; Brookhaven National Laboratory: Upton, NY. nndc.bnl.gov/nudat3Used for: gamma-ray energies and half-lives (60Co, 137Cs, 99mTc, 22Na, 24Na, 40K).
  9. NASA Science. Tour of the Electromagnetic Spectrum. science.nasa.gov/emsUsed for: the overview of the regions and their natural sources.
  10. Klingelhöfer, G.; Morris, R. V.; Bernhardt, B.; et al. Jarosite and Hematite at Meridiani Planum from Opportunity’s Mössbauer Spectrometer. Science 2004, 306 (5702), 1740–1745.Used for: Mössbauer spectroscopy on Mars.
